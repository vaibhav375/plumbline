Plumbline is live and works end to end from a public link: paste or upload content, get cited findings and a "not checked" list, accept approved-wording fixes, pre-check privately, submit, review with routed approvals, and verify the audit trail. 25 labelled posts (33 problems), 10 clean controls, 5 prompt-injection copies; written for this project, labels frozen before any model run. A finding counts only with the right rule or fact and the right words. Baseline: one model call per post with the whole brand book, no validators.

A negative result. "Citations that check out" was 100% for both systems: the baseline cites real IDs and real quotes, just the wrong ones. Precision is what separates them. Two of Plumbline's 8 false alarms are plain errors.

Tests. 240 checker unit tests, 17 database tests (row-level security, audit chain, second approver), 18 end-to-end tests run against the live site.

Speed on free tiers. Text checks 1.8–3.6 s; image checks about 6 s, over the 5 s target because OCR alone takes ~3.8 s on the shared CPU. The first check after a restart fell from ~20 s to 4.6 s by loading the privacy model at startup.

Build log. Every one of the 24 tasks was evaluated against real runs, and each assumption that failed is recorded with what replaced it (docs/build-log.md).

Three improvements for Round 3

Claim recall and live-post watch

When a fact expires or changes, every published asset using it gets an update-or-withdraw task; published URLs are re-checked against the approved version; claims inside images are checked.Measured on the human-labelled held-out set and ASCI decisions

Fixes where creators work

Slack results, a no-login agency link with expiry and rate limits, a publish-gate API, and DOCX tracked changes.0 invented claims in a 200-fix audit; ≥ 70% of fixes accepted by real writers

Self-learning guidelines and confidential mode

Automated rule graduation with undo, AI-drafted clarifications from real decisions, and a self-hosted open-weight Qwen mode.Top-5 "may be unclear" includes ≥ 2 of testers' 3 most confusing rules
